# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Dubai residents, primarily women 25–45, buying flowers as occasion gifts (birthdays, anniversaries, romantic gestures) or as an elevated everyday indulgence. They are mobile-first, high purchasing power, accustomed to luxury retail experiences. They arrive with intent — something happened, someone matters, and they want the gift to reflect that. They expect the site itself to feel like the gift.

## Product Purpose

Urban Rose / Flower Dubai is a premium flower and gift delivery service operating across Dubai. The site is the entire brand experience: it must seduce before it sells. Success looks like a visitor who lingers, feels the quality through the screen, and orders without second-guessing the price.

## Brand Personality

Formal, luxurious, immersive. The brand speaks with restraint and confidence — never loud, never eager. Like a high-end florist in a hotel lobby: quiet competence, exquisite product, no need to shout.

## Anti-references

- Garden Dreams / Bloom Haven tier: pink bokeh gradients, sparkle overlays, scalloped wave dividers, icon grids with lorem ipsum, template-flower-shop energy
- Generic "feminine pink" SaaS landing pages
- Canva-style promotional banners with drop shadows and starburst badges
- Any layout that could belong to a $30 supermarket bouquet

## Design Principles

1. **The product is the hero.** Every layout decision exists to make the flowers look extraordinary. Typography, spacing, and color defer to the photography.
2. **Restraint signals luxury.** More whitespace than feels comfortable. Fewer elements per section. One thing at a time.
3. **Immersion over information.** The site should feel like stepping into a room, not reading a menu. Sections transition like scenes.
4. **Formal confidence.** No exclamation points in spirit or in code. Large, unhurried type. No bouncing, no sparkle, no urgency tricks.
5. **Dubai light.** Warm, golden, late-afternoon. Not cool-clinical, not dark-moody. The palette evokes the city at its most beautiful hour.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Reduced motion respected via `prefers-reduced-motion`. Sufficient contrast on all text over image overlays. Arabic-script readiness considered in layout (right-to-left spacing should not break anything structurally).
